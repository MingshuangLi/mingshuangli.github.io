Mingshuang Li is an Assistant Professor and Dispensing Audiologist in the Department of Communication Disorders and Sciences at California State University, Northridge. His research focuses on speech perception and speech enhancement under degraded listening conditions, integrating psychoacoustic methods, signal processing, auditory electrophysiology, and clinical practice and supervision. He also serves as a topic editor, guest editor, and peer reviewer for scholarly journals.

At CSUN, he serves as Supervising Faculty of the Hearing Science Laboratory, teaches across the Au.D. curriculum, advises Au.D. capstone research, and provides clinical supervision in diagnostic, electrophysiological, and vestibular audiology. He previously served as Interim Director of the Au.D. program and contributes to student professional development.

Research

Behavioral, neural, and translational approaches

Program vision

How can behavioral, neural, and signal-processing approaches improve speech perception under degraded listening conditions?

Primary research area

Speech enhancement

Developing targeted signal-processing strategies to improve speech perception under degraded listening conditions, including background noise, face-mask attenuation, and hearing loss.

Selected topics: Formant enhancement for natural and N95-muffled speech; combined F2–F3 enhancement; effects of noise type, signal-to-noise ratio, and listener characteristics.

Research foundation

Speech perception & psychoacoustics

Examining how acoustic cues, language experience, and auditory processing shape speech and pitch perception.

Selected topics: Vowel and consonant perception in noise, formant-cue sensitivity, masking release, pitch processing, and auditory processing in congenital amusia.

Growing research area

Auditory electrophysiology

Using auditory brainstem responses and frequency-following responses to investigate the neural encoding of speech and complex sounds.

Selected topics: Effects of speech content and spectral and temporal modulation on cABR/FFR; development of clinically relevant electrophysiological measures.
